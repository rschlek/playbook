# Define is an open interview that ends with a problem brief

Question: How does a define interview go, from a stated want to a picked option?

Replaced in part by: 2026-11-20-problem-definition-is-a-file-in-the-project.md. The document is called a problem definition.

It does not end in a picked option. `define` is an open interview that gets to the root of a problem and ends with a written problem brief. Ways to solve the problem are found in a later step.

## What was decided

1. An open interview. `define` questions the user about the problem the way `grill-me` questions a plan: one question at a time, following the branches that matter, looking up what the project itself can answer, until both understand the problem the same way. There is no fixed list of questions. The ground it covers: who has the problem, what happens today, what it costs, and how anyone would know it is solved.
2. It ends with a document. The result is a problem brief, in the manner of a business requirements document. "Problem brief" is a working name. It holds: the problem in a few plain sentences; who has it and who else is affected; what happens today; what it costs; what solved looks like, in a way someone could check; limits such as budget, deadline, and approvals; what is out of scope; the idea that started the conversation; and what is still unknown, each with who could answer it.
3. No solutions. `define` lays out no options and picks nothing. The solution the user arrived with is recorded as the idea that started it and is not judged.
4. Research only where the problem is unclear. `define` proposes what it would look up and where, marks the sources that are internal to the user's organization, and starts only on the user's yes. It uses the `research` skill. Looking at what already exists to solve the problem belongs to the later step.
5. What it cannot reach. A source the session cannot read is not guessed at. The item goes under what is still unknown, with who could answer it.
6. The user sees the brief first. `define` shows the whole brief and corrects it until the user says it is the problem. Nothing is written before that yes.
7. Finished with open questions. When some unknowns can only be answered by other people, the brief is still written, marked as a draft with open questions, and updated when the answers come back. Taking those questions to the people named is the role of the questionnaire skill.

## Options considered

- A short fixed procedure of about four questions: not taken. The problem is found by following what matters, not by working through a list.
- `define` always lays out options and records a pick: not taken.
- `define` writes the brief and then offers to go on to options: taken at first, then not taken. A problem written with a recommended solution beside it gets bent to fit the solution, and a brief without one can be handed to someone else or sent out for comment.
- Research started without asking: not taken. The user sees what would be looked up, and which sources are internal, before it starts.
- The brief waits until every unknown is answered: not taken. Other people can take days, and a draft is what they would be shown anyway.

## What settled it

- A walk-through on an invented example, which the person reacted to scene by scene.
- The person's view: this step is about getting to the root of the problem, and another process is in charge of the solution.

## Not settled here

- The brief's name, where it is kept, and how it reaches `wayfinder` as the destination. A separate question on the map holds these. A brief can hold facts internal to an organization, so where it is kept matters most in a shared or public repository.
- What turns a problem brief into a chosen way to solve it, and where the small options are looked at. A new question holds it.
- What the questionnaire skill is called and how its answers come back. A separate question holds it.

## Replaces

In part, the decision that define is its own skill (`2026-11-20-define-is-its-own-skill.md`): `define` does not look at what already exists, lay out options, or record a pick. That it is its own skill, and that it starts from a want where `grill-me` starts from a plan, stands.
