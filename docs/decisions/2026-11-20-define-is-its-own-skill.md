# Define is its own skill

Question: Is define its own skill or a mode of grill-me?

Replaced in part by: 2026-11-20-define-ends-with-a-problem-brief.md. `define` lays out no options and records no pick; it ends with a problem brief.

The problem-definition step is a skill of its own, named `define`. It is not a mode of `grill-me`. The two interview one question at a time and point in opposite directions.

## What was decided

1. Its own skill, named `define`.
2. What tells the two apart for a user:
   - `define` starts from a want. It asks whether the thing asked for is the answer, looks at what already exists, lays out options that include the small ones, and records what the user picked. Use it before there is a plan: "I want X", "should we build X", "what is the real problem here", "is this worth doing".
   - `grill-me` starts from a plan the user already has and tests it. It looks for no alternatives and records nothing. Use it when there is a plan: "grill me", "stress-test this", "what am I missing".
3. In `guide-me`, a loose want with no map and no plan leads to `define`. Until `define` exists, that row keeps pointing at `grill-me`.
4. Each skill names the other when the user is in the wrong one: `grill-me` when the plan's premise turns out to be the open question, `define` when the user already has a plan and wants it tested. Neither starts the other without the user's yes.

## Options considered

- A mode of `grill-me`: not taken. One name would mean two opposite things, and `grill-me` records nothing while the problem-definition step has to record its result.
- Its own skill: taken.

## What settled it

- The two do different work: `define` looks outward, lays out options, and leaves a record, and `grill-me` does none of these.
- `guide-me` offers one skill by name for each state of the work, so the first step needs a name of its own.

## Not settled here

- How a `define` interview goes, from a stated want to a picked option. A separate question on the map holds it.
- Where the result is recorded, and how it reaches `wayfinder` as the destination. A separate question holds it.
- The questionnaire skill for answers that belong to someone else. A separate question holds it.
